# Inventing a look

This repository ships no look, on purpose. The person you work for should end up with films that look like theirs, not like anyone else's, and that only happens if you decide the look for each film, write the decisions down, and keep the ones they like.

Write `films/<name>/look.md` before you draw a frame. Copy `templates/look.md`.

## What a look decides

- **Palette.** Named colours and what each one means, kept to for the whole film.
- **Type.** One or two faces that are on the machine or embedded in the scene. Sizes for a card, a label and a caption.
- **Ground.** What is behind everything: a colour, a gradient, a drawn place, an image you made.
- **Motion.** How things enter and leave, how fast, how they ease. What moves while the rest holds still.
- **Light.** Whether there is glow, bloom, grain or texture, and where it is never used.
- **What the look refuses.** Two or three things this look will never do. These are often the most useful lines in the file.
- **Where it came from.** If the look borrows from something, name what was borrowed (a palette, an era, a medium) and keep the source's logos, marks and names out. Borrow a look, never a brand.

## Questions the look must answer

The end of `protocol/lessons.md` lists questions that came out of many films. Each was answered once for one studio's taste, and the answers are left out on purpose. Answer them for this look in your `look.md`.

## Keeping what works

When the person likes a look, keep its `look.md` somewhere your next film can find it, with their words about it. A look they liked is the start of their house style. A look they rejected is worth keeping too, with the reason, so you do not make it again.
